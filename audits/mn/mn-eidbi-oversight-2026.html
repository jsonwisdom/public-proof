Minnesota: What Did DHS Have Authority to Investigate?

A March 2026 legislative review disputes the Department of Human Services' interpretation of its power to address alleged kickbacks. The records raise an oversight question—not a blanket verdict against providers.

BLACK BAR PRESS · Research framework: Jason Wisdom · State-switch pilot · November 20, 2026

Minnesota's Office of the Legislative Auditor released a special review on March 17, 2026, titled Department of Human Services Investigations of Alleged Kickbacks in the Early Intensive Developmental and Behavioral Intervention Program. The report addressed the agency's treatment of complaints involving EIDBI services.

Authority versus interpretation

According to the auditor, DHS officials asserted that, before 2025, the department lacked authority to investigate kickback allegations unless the complaint also described conduct fitting other defined categories. The auditor disagreed and concluded DHS had long possessed legal authority to address alleged kickbacks. The auditor also identified a decades-old administrative-rule error that may restrict the department's ability to suspend payments while investigating.

CI-FIRST rule: A complaint is not a conviction. A disagreement over agency authority is not itself proof of a criminal kickback. The public can test the legal interpretation against the report, statutes, rules and documented agency decisions.

Jason's Minnesota audit method

Jay Wisdom's Minnesota public-records work places legal authority, original data and documented action in separate lanes. This pilot applies that method to the official OLA review rather than treating allegations as findings. Minnesota's Data Practices Act (Chapter 13) supplies the public-record access framework; it does not make legally protected records automatically public.

What closes the gaps?

For each disputed action, establish the governing statute or rule, complaint date, agency disposition, reasons supplied, payment or sanction decision, and verifiable source record. Preserve original files, compute hashes, and record when a document cannot lawfully be disclosed. Published state findings and unresolved original-record access must not be combined into a fabricated case conclusion.
